Listen to any page or your notes. Changes save as you make them.

API key

Read-a-Laud speaks through Fish Audio, and you bring your own key.

The key is stored locally in chrome.storage.local and is only ever sent to api.fish.audio. It is never written to a log and stays masked until you press Show. Get a key at fish.audio

Model

Fish bills per million UTF-8 bytes of text sent, not per minute of audio.

A 5,000-character English article is roughly 5,000 UTF-8 bytes, which costs $0.075 on s2.1-pro and $0 on s2.1-pro-free. Read twenty of those a day and the paid model runs about $1.50 a day, near $135 across a 90-day term. Worth choosing deliberately.

Voice

Browse Fish's public voices, or paste an ID straight from a voice page.

Open any voice on fish.audio and copy the ID from its URL. Leave this empty to use the Fish default voice.

Searching needs a working API key.

An honest note on Indian languages. Fish's officially documented language list (13 languages) does not include Hindi, Tamil, Telugu or any other Indian language. Indian-accented English voices are the reliable case here. Hindi and other Indian-language output depends on community-trained voices, and quality varies from excellent to unusable — so preview a voice on a real sentence before you commit to it for a whole textbook.

    Reading

    What gets read out loud, and how it sounds.

    Applied during playback, so changing it costs nothing and takes effect immediately.

    Gain applied at synthesis, in decibels. 0 dB is the voice as recorded.

    How much text goes into each request. Smaller chunks start playing sooner but break sentences apart, so the rhythm gets choppier and pauses land in odd places. Larger chunks sound more natural and hold their pacing across a paragraph, but you wait longer before the first word. Around 900 characters is a comfortable middle.

    Cache

    Audio you have already heard is kept on disk.

    Because Fish bills per byte of text sent, replaying a cached page costs nothing — re-listening to yesterday's chapter before an exam is free. Clearing the cache frees disk space, but the next play of those pages will be synthesized and billed again.
